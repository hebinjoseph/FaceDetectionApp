Academic Theory & Methodology

How Face Detection Works: Computer Vision Fundamentals

A technical breakdown of modern and classical facial localization algorithms.

1. Viola-Jones Haar Cascade (Classical)

OpenCV Native

Proposed by Paul Viola and Michael Jones in 2001, this revolutionized real-time face detection prior to modern GPUs.

  • Haar-like Features: Rectangular pixel difference patterns that mimic facial features (e.g., eye regions are darker than cheekbones).
  • Integral Image: Constant-time \(O(1)\) area summation technique allowing rapid feature calculation across arbitrary window scales.
  • AdaBoost: Selects the top critical features from tens of thousands of candidates to form weak classifiers.
  • Cascade Architecture: Rejects non-face background windows early in the pipeline, optimizing compute efficiency.

2. BlazeFace & SSD (Modern Deep Learning)

MediaPipe / This App

Google's sub-millisecond neural network specifically architected for mobile and browser edge computing.

  • Single Shot Detector (SSD): Evaluates image feature maps in a single neural forward pass rather than sliding multi-scale windows.
  • Modified Convolution Kernels: Employs 5x5 depthwise separable convolutions to expand receptive field while maintaining low floating-point operations (FLOPs).
  • 6 3D Facial Landmarks: Predicts eye centers, nose tip, mouth center, and ear tragions alongside 2D bounding boxes.
  • Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS): Merges overlapping candidate bounding boxes based on Intersection-over-Union (IoU) metrics.

Mathematical Metric: Intersection over Union (IoU)

During face localization, candidate bounding boxes \(B_1\) and \(B_2\) are evaluated using the Jaccard Index (IoU) to filter redundant detections:

IoU = Area(B_1 ∩ B_2) / Area(B_1 ∪ B_2)

Detections exceeding an IoU overlap threshold (typically 0.45) with a higher-confidence candidate are suppressed during the Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS) stage.
